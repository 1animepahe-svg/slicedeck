THE DETAILS

Good questions.

The quick answers, without the fine print.

Does SliceDeck download YouTube videos by itself?

No. The preview uses YouTube's embedded player. Exporting a YouTube clip requires a separate compatible backend that you run or provide. GitHub Pages hosts static files and cannot do the processing.

Can I clip my own video without a backend?

Yes, if your browser supports captureStream and MediaRecorder. The local editor records a WebM clip in real time; it may not work on all browsers or phones, and audio support varies.

Which videos can I use?

Only use material you own or have permission to use, and follow applicable platform terms and laws. Some YouTube videos cannot be embedded or processed.

Why can’t I connect to my backend?

The backend must be running, reachable from your device and configured for cross-origin requests. An HTTPS site may be blocked from using an HTTP backend. The default 127.0.0.1 address means the device you are using.

Are local exports instant?

No. A 30-second selection takes about 30 seconds to record. Keep the tab open and avoid interrupting playback. The result may not be frame-accurate.

Leave a beat after the main moment so the clip doesn’t feel abruptly cut off.

Check your rights and the platform's rules before saving or sharing someone else's work.

PRIVACY

Know where
your video goes.

Local editor

Files selected in the local editor are processed in your browser and are not uploaded by this app. Exported clips are temporary in the browser until you download them or leave the page.

YouTube previews

Loading a preview connects to YouTube's privacy-enhanced embed service. YouTube may receive technical information such as your IP address and use data according to its own policies.

Backend exports

When you request a YouTube clip, your video URL and timestamps are sent to the backend address you provide. That backend handles its own data, logs and retention. Use a backend you trust.

Storage

This frontend does not create accounts, use analytics or save files to a server. Your browser and any external services may handle data independently.
